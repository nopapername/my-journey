ARTICLE 6
TITLE 陆家嘴 - 上海金融中心摩天大楼天际线 - LoongWander
URL https://www.loongwander.com/zh-CN/article/lujiazui
陆家嘴位于上海市浦东新区黄浦江畔，是上海的金融中心与地标性文旅集聚区。陆家嘴地理范围东至浦东南路、环路，南至东昌路，西、北临黄浦江，总面积约1.7平方公里。作为上海浦东开发开放的核心地标，陆家嘴以密集的超高层建筑群、完善的金融服务体系和独特的滨江景观为核心特色，拥有多座高度位列全球前列的摩天大楼，其中上海中心大厦总高度632米，为中国第一、世界第三高楼；上海环球金融中心高度492米，金茂大厦高度420.5米，三座建筑共同构成“陆家嘴三件套”城市天际线。截至2025年，陆家嘴区域集聚了超千家金融机构、数十家跨国公司地区总部，同时融合了滨江休闲、都市观光、文化体验等多元旅游功能，年接待游客超千万人次，是上海都市旅游的核心名片。
历史文化
陆家嘴的名称源于明代翰林院学士陆深，其家族曾在此聚居，且此地位于黄浦江转弯处，形似“嘴”状，故而得名。在近代，陆家嘴曾是上海的轻工业聚集地和码头仓储区，见证了上海近代工业的起步与发展，留存有少量近代工业建筑遗迹，成为浦东历史脉络的重要佐证。
1990年，上海浦东开发开放战略正式启动，陆家嘴被确立为国家级金融贸易区，自此开启了从传统工业区向国际金融中心的转型之路。三十余年间，陆家嘴从一片农田与旧厂房区域，逐步建成集金融、贸易、旅游、文化于一体的现代化城区，其发展历程成为中国改革开放的生动缩影，也形成了“敢闯敢试、敢为人先”的浦东精神，成为陆家嘴独特的文化内核。
如今，陆家嘴在保留金融核心功能的同时，不断融入海派文化与现代都市文化，滨江大道的城市公共空间、各类艺术展馆、文化展演活动等，让陆家嘴不仅是金融高地，更是上海文化交流的重要平台，实现了经济与文化的双向赋能。
主要景点
上海中心大厦
上海中心大厦坐落于陆家嘴银城中路501号，总建筑面积57.8万平方米，建筑总高度632米，由美国Gensler建筑设计事务所设计，2016年正式投入使用。大厦共118层（地上），地下5层，集办公、酒店、观光、商业等功能于一体，其顶部的“上海之巅”观光厅位于118-119层，海拔546米，配备全球最快的超高速电梯（速度达18米/秒），仅需55秒即可从地下1层抵达观光层，游客可360度俯瞰上海全景，是陆家嘴登高观光的核心景点。
上海中心大厦
上海环球金融中心
上海环球金融中心位于陆家嘴世纪大道100号，建筑高度492米，地上101层，地下3层，总建筑面积38.16万平方米，2008年正式开业。其最具标志性的设计是顶部的“风洞”造型，94层（423米）、97层（439米）和100层（474米）设有观光设施，其中100层的悬空观光长廊采用透明玻璃地面，游客可直面脚下的城市景观，是陆家嘴极具特色的高空观光体验点，大厦同时承载国际金融办公、商业配套等功能。
上海环球金融中心
金茂大厦
金茂大厦位于陆家嘴世纪大道88号，总高度420.5米，地上88层，地下3层，总建筑面积29万平方米，1999年建成开业，是陆家嘴早期的超高层地标建筑。大厦融合了中国传统宝塔建筑风格与现代摩天楼设计，88层观光厅高度340.1米，拥有开放式的高空观景平台，可近距离观赏黄浦江两岸风光；大厦内还设有金茂君悦大酒店，占据53至87层，是世界上最高的酒店之一，成为商务与观光结合的典范。
金茂大厦
陆家嘴滨江大道
陆家嘴滨江大道西起东昌路渡口，东至浦东南路，全长约2.5公里，占地面积约10万平方米，是集休闲、观光、生态于一体的城市滨江公共空间。滨江大道紧邻黄浦江，与外滩万国建筑博览群隔江相望，沿途设置了亲水平台、观景步道、绿化景观带及各类休闲设施，游客可步行欣赏黄浦江景与陆家嘴天际线，也是拍摄上海城市景观的热门地点，每日开放时间为全天，是陆家嘴市民与游客休闲的核心区域。
陆家嘴滨江大道
上海海洋水族馆
上海海洋水族馆位于陆家嘴环路1388号，总建筑面积约20500平方米，是国家AAAA级旅游景区，2002年正式对外开放。馆内拥有长达155米的海底隧道，为世界最长的海底观光隧道之一，展示了来自全球五大洲、四大洋的超500种、超10000尾水生生物，包括中华鲟、扬子鳄等珍稀物种，是集展示、科普、教育于一体的海洋主题展馆，年接待游客超200万人次。
上海海洋水族馆
上海迪士尼旗舰店
上海迪士尼旗舰店（陆家嘴店）位于陆家嘴丰和路180号，是迪士尼在中国大陆的首家旗舰店，建筑面积约5000平方米，店内汇集了迪士尼全系列主题商品，从经典卡通形象周边到限量版纪念品，是亲子游玩与迪士尼粉丝打卡的热门地点，同时建筑外观融入迪士尼经典元素，成为陆家嘴商业文旅的特色节点。

游玩路线
推荐陆家嘴一日经典游玩路线：上午9:00抵达陆家嘴地铁站（2号线），首先前往上海海洋水族馆（游玩约2小时），近距离观赏海洋生物；11:30前往滨江大道，沿黄浦江步行至东昌路渡口段，欣赏外滩与陆家嘴天际线，途中可在滨江休闲区稍作休息，享用简餐；下午13:30前往金茂大厦88层观光厅（游玩约1小时），体验高空观景；15:00前往上海环球金融中心，打卡100层悬空观光长廊（游玩约1.5小时）；16:30前往上海中心大厦，登顶“上海之巅”观光厅（游玩约1小时），欣赏日落时分的上海全景；18:00后可前往陆家嘴中心L+Mall或正大广场享用晚餐，餐后可在滨江大道观赏外滩夜景，结束当日行程。若时间充裕，可增加迪士尼旗舰店、陆家嘴中心绿地等点位，调整为两日游玩路线，首日侧重高空观光与滨江景观，次日侧重海洋馆与商业体验。
游玩建议
最佳游玩时间：春秋两季（3-5月、9-11月），气温适宜，能见度高，适合登高观景；若想观赏夜景，建议选择周五、周六晚间，外滩及陆家嘴灯光秀效果更佳，灯光开放时间为18:00-22:30。
登高观光建议：上海中心大厦、环球金融中心、金茂大厦任选1-2座即可，避免重复体验；建议提前1-2天在官方平台购票，可节省现场排队时间。
亲子游玩建议：优先选择上海海洋水族馆+迪士尼旗舰店的组合，海洋水族馆建议游玩时长2-3小时，避开周末及节假日高峰，每日9:00开馆时入场人流量较少。
摄影建议：拍摄陆家嘴天际线最佳点位为滨江大道东昌路渡口附近，拍摄外滩与陆家嘴同框最佳时间为日落前1小时（“黄金时刻”）；拍摄高空全景建议携带广角镜头，上海中心118层观光厅允许使用非专业摄影设备。
餐饮建议：陆家嘴区域餐饮价格偏高，可选择正大广场、陆家嘴中心L+Mall等综合商场就餐，滨江区域也有简餐咖啡店。
游玩时长：核心景点全覆盖建议1天（8小时），若包含深度体验（如酒店下午茶、展馆细逛）建议1.5-2天。
注意事项
登高观光注意：上海中心、环球金融中心、金茂大厦的观光厅均禁止携带易燃易爆物品、大件行李（超过20寸行李箱），玻璃栈道区域禁止穿高跟鞋踩踏，儿童需由成人陪同，1.2米以下儿童部分场馆免票。
人流管控：节假日（如春节、国庆）陆家嘴核心区域会实施人流管控，部分路段可能临时封闭，建议提前关注浦东新区文旅局公告，错峰出行。
交通提示：陆家嘴地铁站（2号线）节假日高峰时段会限流，建议选择14号线浦东南路站、9号线商城路站出站后步行前往，避免拥堵。
天气提示：登高观景受天气影响较大，若遇雾霾、大雨天气，能见度会大幅降低，建议出行前查询空气质量指数（AQI），AQI低于100时观景效果最佳。
财物安全：陆家嘴游客密集区域，需注意保管手机、钱包等随身物品，滨江大道等开阔区域谨防无人机、风筝等高空物品坠落风险。
设施使用：滨江大道的公共座椅、卫生间等设施免费使用，禁止占用公共空间摆摊、露营，遵守公共场所管理规定。
交通
地铁：乘坐上海地铁2号线至陆家嘴站（1号、2号、6号口出站均可直达核心区域）；14号线至浦东南路站（4号口出站，步行约800米至滨江大道）；9号线至商城路站（1号口出站，步行约1公里至陆家嘴中心区域）。
公交：陆家嘴区域公交线路有81路、82路、583路、799路、939路、961路等，陆家嘴环路丰和路站、陆家嘴地铁站站为核心公交站点，可直达各景点。
轮渡：从浦西外滩金陵东路渡口乘坐东金线轮渡至东昌路渡口，渡江约10分钟，上岸后步行5分钟即可到达滨江大道，是体验黄浦江交通的特色方式。
自驾：陆家嘴核心区域停车场主要有正大广场停车场、上海中心大厦停车场、金茂大厦停车场，节假日停车位紧张，建议优先选择公共交通。
出租车/网约车：陆家嘴区域网约车下车点主要集中在正大广场南门、上海中心大厦东门、滨江大道东昌路入口处，禁止在主干道随意停车上下客。

The Bund, Shanghai: Maps, How to Visit, and What to See
 Written by

 Updated Aug. 31, 2026 
The Bund is Shanghai's most iconic landmark, and if it's your first visit, give yourself 2–3 hours if you want both the historic buildings and the skyline; 30–60 minutes is enough for a quick photo stop.
For the classic night scene, arrive before sunset and watch the city lights come on gradually, enjoying the sharp contrast between day and night. 
If you value open space and a calmer pace over close-up detail, the North Bund offers a quieter skyline view, but you'll miss some of the intimate Bund atmosphere.
Main Bund or North Bund: Which One Should You Choose?
The Main Bund and the North Bund offer two very different ways to experience Shanghai's waterfront.
For most first-time visitors, we recommend the Main Bund.
The Main Bund is the famous stretch along Zhongshan East 1st Road, where you walk beside Shanghai's historic waterfront buildings — the architectural collection often referred to as the "Museum of World Architecture." Across the Huangpu River is the Pudong skyline.
The North Bund sits further north, around the Huangpu River and Suzhou Creek. It gives you more space, fewer people, and wider views back toward both Pudong and the historic Bund.
So the choice is simple:
Choose the Main Bund if you want to get close to Shanghai's historic buildings and understand the stories behind them.
Choose the North Bund if photography, open space, and skyline views matter more to you than architectural history.

The Bund
Time needed: 2–3 hours
Best for: First-time visitors, history and architecture lovers, families, and couples
If this is your first time in Shanghai, this is the route we recommend.
The Main Bund is not just about standing by the river and taking the classic skyline photo. The real experience is in the buildings behind you.
Along this stretch are Shanghai's famous historic waterfront buildings, built in dramatically different architectural styles. But unless you know what you are looking at, it is easy to walk past them without understanding why they matter.

This is the best route if you want to understand why the Bund is important, rather than simply take a photo of it. And that is how we design the rest of our Shanghai tours too — not simply taking you to see the city, but helping you experience it and understand the stories behind it.

North Bund Walk: Better for Space and Photography
Shanghai North Bund with Little Egg
Time needed: 1.5–2 hours
Best for: Photographers, repeat visitors, and travelers who prefer fewer crowds
The North Bund is where we would send you if you have already seen the Main Bund — or if you care more about photography than getting close to the historic buildings.
The waterfront here is more open, with fewer crowds and more room to walk.
You also get several different angles for photographing the Pudong skyline, the historic Bund buildings, and the Huangpu River. Instead of walking directly among the historic architecture, you are looking back toward it from a greater distance.
That makes the North Bund a better choice if your priority is wide skyline shots rather than architectural details and historical stories.
It can also be a smart alternative during extremely busy travel periods, such as the Labour Day holiday or National Day Golden Week, when the Main Bund can become particularly crowded.
Our route: Shanghai Postal Museum → Garden Bridge → North Bund waterfront → Little Egg → riverside viewpoints
This is a looser walk built around viewpoints rather than a historical narrative. You can stop where the views interest you rather than following a strict sightseeing sequence.
However, for most first-time visitors, we still would not choose the North Bund instead of the Main Bund.
You may get more space and more photo opportunities, but you lose the chance to walk directly among the buildings that made the Bund famous.

When Is the Best Time to Visit the Bund?
Arrive Around 2 PM: Get the Full Bund Experience (Most Recommended)
In-depth tour of the Bund in Shanghai
This is our top recommendation if you are visiting the Bund for the first time.
This gives you enough daylight to see the historic buildings properly, rather than viewing them only as illuminated facades at night. You can spend around 2.5 hours on an in-depth walk, then take a break for afternoon tea before returning to the waterfront as the sky begins to change.
By staying until after sunset, you get both sides of the Bund in one visit: the architectural details and stories that are easiest to appreciate during the day, followed by the Pudong skyline as the lights gradually come on.
Arrive 30–60 Minutes Before Sunset: Best for the Skyline Night View
Watching the sunset on the Bund in Shanghai
If the Pudong skyline is all you really want to see, there is no need to spend the entire afternoon at the Bund.
Arrive around 30–60 minutes before sunset and stay until the city lights are fully on.
The advantage is not just seeing Shanghai at night. You get to watch the scene change from daylight to dusk, then to a fully illuminated skyline. This transition is what creates the dramatic contrast behind many of the Bund's most recognizable views.
As a rough guide, sunset is usually around 6:30–7:00 PM in summer and 5:00–5:30 PM in winter, although the exact time changes throughout the year.
If you arrive only after dark, the view is still impressive — but you will miss the transition.
Go Around 8:30 AM on a Weekday: Best for Avoiding Crowds
On a weekday morning, the Bund in Shanghai is sparsely populated.
Around 8:30 AM on a weekday, traffic is light, the promenade is nearly empty, and you can walk or jog along the river with almost no one around.
However, most historic buildings won't be open yet, so you may need to wait half an hour to an hour before embarking cultural walking tour, or you want the exterior views and a quiet atmosphere.

FAQs About the Bund
Do I Need to Take a Huangpu River Cruise?
Huangpu River cruise at night for the Bund
Time needed: about 45–60 minutes
Best time: 7 PM departures, when the lights start on, and the atmosphere is at its best
Best for: Families, couples, photographers, and visitors who want the skyline without walking the entire waterfront
We recommend taking a Huangpu River cruise at night.
The Bund promenade gives you the classic view toward Pudong, but from the river, you can see both sides of Shanghai at once.
On one side are the historic buildings of the Bund. On the other is Pudong's modern skyline.
Seeing the two riverbanks from the water gives you a much clearer sense of the contrast that defines Shanghai: its colonial-era architecture facing one of the world's most futuristic skylines.
It is also a much more relaxed way to experience the waterfront. Instead of competing for space on a crowded promenade or walking the entire length of the Bund, you can simply sit back and watch the skyline unfold around you.
Our preferred option is the classic route departing from Shiliupu Marina, which takes you past many of Shanghai's most recognizable waterfront landmarks.
Tips
If it is raining heavily, foggy, or visibility is poor, we would reconsider.
The main reason to take the cruise is the view, and if the skyline is hidden by mist or low clouds, much of the experience is lost.
Is the Bund Free?
Yes. The Main Bund waterfront promenade is free.
You do not need a ticket to walk beside the river or photograph the historic buildings from outside.
Several museums and public interiors are also free, including the China Securities Museum and Shanghai Postal Museum, subject to their opening hours and admission arrangements.
However, a Huangpu River cruise requires payment, and the price depends on the pier, boat, route, and ticket type.
So if your budget is tight, you can only experience the Bund without paying for a cruise.

Jade Buddha Temple, Shanghai: What to See and How to Visit
 Written by
 Carper
 Updated Sep. 24, 2026 
Jade Buddha Temple was built around 1918 in the style of the Song Dynasty (960-1279). Named for its 1.9-meter-high white jade Buddha, Jade Buddha Temple is worth about 1–1.5 hours if you want to see the temple's most prized treasure and experience an active Buddhist temple.
If you are on a tight schedule, or your priority is the Bund, Shanghai's old neighborhoods, or the city's modern skyline, you can skip it.
Visit it if you want:
to see Shanghai's famous jade Buddhas;
to experience an active Buddhist temple;
to learn more about Chinese Buddhist culture;
or to spend some time with traditional temple architecture.
Skip it if you want:
Shanghai's skyline and modern cityscape;
old neighborhoods and cafés;
or a very short list of the city's highest-priority sights.
What to See Inside Jade Buddha Temple
Jade Buddha Temple
Address: 170 Anyuan Road, Putuo District, Shanghai
Opening hours: 8:00–16:30 on regular days
Admission: Free
Metro: Line 13, Jiangning Road Station, Exit 3
Walk from the station: About 5 minutes
Recommended visit: 1–1.5 hours
1. The Two Jade Buddhas: The Absolute Must-see
The white jade Buddha of Jade Buddha Temple
The temple is named after two white jade Buddhas brought to Shanghai from Myanmar in the late 19th century.
The most important stop is the Jade Buddha Hall, where you can see the seated jade Buddha, about 1.9 meters high. Then continue to the Reclining Buddha Hall to see the smaller reclining jade Buddha, which represents the Buddha before entering nirvana.
If you only have one hour, prioritize these two halls. You don't need to inspect every hall or Buddhist statue to understand why the temple is significant.
2. Traditional Chinese Temple Architecture
Roof decorations of the Jade Buddha Temple
The jade Buddhas are the main attraction, but the temple itself is also worth noticing.
As you walk through the complex, look for the symmetrical layout, yellow walls, traditional rooflines, courtyards, and carved decorative details.
The architecture gives you a very different side of Shanghai from the skyscrapers around the Bund and Lujiazui.
If you enjoy Chinese architecture, allow a little extra time to walk rather than rushing directly from one Buddha hall to the next.
Photography Tips
If you want photos, focus on the architecture, courtyards, roof details, and overall temple atmosphere rather than trying to photograph every hall.
Photography is restricted in certain worship areas or during religious activities. Follow the signs inside the temple.
3. Taste the Vegetarian Cuisine
The temple also has a vegetarian food section serving traditional Buddhist vegetarian dishes with Shanghai-style cooking (priced at 50–70 RMB per person).
This works well for vegans, or if you want to eat immediately after visiting rather than finding another restaurant.
I would treat the vegetarian food as an optional add-on, not the main reason to visit. Come for the jade Buddhas first.

We're often asked when the best time is to visit Shanghai.

Autumn and Spring: Your Top Choices
Wukang Mansion in Autumn, Shanghai, China
Autumn (September to November) is the best season to travel to Shanghai. 
Summer crowds, the rainy season, and heatwaves begin to fade, prices return to normal, and the weather is perfect for leisurely city walks.
For more stable weather, plan your trip for later than mid-September, and check the forecast before you go. Typhoons can affect Shanghai from July to September, but the probability drops sharply after mid-September.
Beyond weather, if you're hoping to see Shanghai's autumn colors, aim for mid-November to mid-December. Due to global warming, this cooler window has become a more reliable time for foliage.

Winter: The Cheapest Time
Lantern Festival at Shanghai City God Temple
Winter (December to February) in Shanghai is cold and damp. It rarely sees snow and mainly experiences rain.
Despite the cold, winter offers two big advantages: costs are generally about 30% lower than normal, and crowds are the thinnest of the year—except around late December (Christmas and New Year celebrations) and early February (Chinese New Year).
Aim for December if winter is your only option, when it is a festive month and the days are not that cold, which means you may still witness Shanghai's beautiful foliage.
As for January and February, the coldest months, it is better to focus on indoor activities with heating, such as admiring ancient bronzes at the Shanghai Museum, marveling at the stunning Jade Buddha at the Jade Buddha Temple, and attending a cooking class in a traditional longtang home.

Summer: The Worst Time Without Strategies
Our clients are taking a boat tour in Zhujiajiao Water Town
Summer (June to August) is Shanghai's hottest, rainiest, and most typhoon-prone season, with average monthly rainfall of 5.2 inches and temperatures around 80°F (27°C).
Early to mid-June is your best window, when the temperatures and crowds haven't peaked yet, and rain is less frequent.
July is the hottest and most crowded month. Expect significant increases in hotel and airfare prices, as well as large crowds at attractions.
August is slightly cooler and less crowded than July, but typhoons are more likely and can affect flights. Be sure to check weather forecasts carefully and plan accordingly.
