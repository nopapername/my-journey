The Complete Guide to Your Layover in Shanghai 
 Written by

 Updated Aug. 6, 2026 
Should you leave the Shanghai Pudong International Airport during a Shanghai layover? The quick answer is: yes, but only if you have 6 hours or more to spend.
Remember: your layover clock shrinks fast. Immigration, baggage, and the round-trip transfer to the city can easily eat up 2–3 hours. You need to be back at the airport at least 1.5 hours before your next flight.
Though we have listed out so many experiences, we still think a successful Shanghai layover is not about checking off as many attractions as possible. It is about choosing the right experience for the time you actually have.
So ask yourself before planning: do you have enough real city time to enjoy Shanghai, or will leaving the airport only turn your layover into a stressful race?

Things to Know Before Leaving the Airport During a Shanghai Layover
If your layover is 6 hours or longer, leaving the airport is absolutely worth it.
However, if it is less than 6 hours, you are not recommended to leave the airport. Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG) has free Wi-Fi, restaurants, and duty-free shops. You will be fully entertained.

2. Do You Need a Visa for a Shanghai Layover?
Shanghai municipality offers the Transit Without Visa (TWOV) policy for travelers, which lets you visit Shanghai visa-free for up to 10 days. See our China Visa-Free Transit Policy page.
Key Conditions
You must be traveling between two different countries/regions (e.g., USA → Shanghai → Japan).
You must have a confirmed onward ticket.
You must enter and exit through Shanghai Pudong (PVG), Shanghai Hongqiao (SHA), or certain other ports.
If you are unsure whether the policy could work for you, check with your airline or the nearest Chinese embassy before traveling.
3. How to Get from Shanghai Pudong Airport (PVG) to the City
Shanghai Maglev Train
Shanghai Pudong Airport is about 45–60 minutes from the city center. Here are your options:
Metro: Take Line 2 to People's Square or Nanjing Road to get to the Bund, or Lujiazui for Shanghai Tower. Trains run every 8 minutes from the airport between 6:00 AM and 10:30 PM. Traveling to People's Square takes about 68 minutes and costs 7 yuan.
Maglev: Experience the world's fastest train, and it takes only 7–8 minutes to travel from the airport to Longyang Road Station, where you can transfer to Metro Line 2 or 7 to the city center. Expect to pay 50 yuan for a one-way trip.
Taxi: You can find taxis outside the arrivals halls of both T1 and T2 terminals. It takes about 1 hour to get to the city center, and maybe 1½–2 hours during rush hour. Typically the cost is about 150–200 yuan for a one-way trip.
Private Transfer: We offer a secure, reliable, and fast private transfer service from Shanghai Pudong International Airport and other ports, starting at a cost of 18 USD. It is the best option for those who are looking for a hassle-free journey with efficiency and flexibility. See the details about our service.
If you have 7–10 hours, a taxi or private transfer saves you the most time. If you have 10+ hours, the metro is fine and gives you a glimpse of local life.
However, if you happen to be traveling during rush hour, choose the Maglev train back to the airport to avoid traffic congestion in case missing your next flight.
4. Do You Have a Payment Method Ready?
People in China are more accustomed to using electronic mobile payments rather than cash.
Nowadays, China is a predominantly cashless society, and international credit cards are not widely accepted outside of luxury hotels and tourist shops. You will need one of the following to pay for taxis, meals, and entrance fees:
Alipay or WeChat Pay: These are the most convenient options, and the two most widely used payment apps in China. You can link your international credit card to either app before you travel.
Cash (RMB): Still accepted everywhere, though you may receive change in coins or small bills. Exchange some currency at the airport arrivals hall—there are multiple currency exchange counters available. Avoid exchanging large amounts; a few hundred RMB is usually enough for a layover.
International credit cards: Widely accepted at major hotels, upscale restaurants, and larger stores. But hard to count on them for taxis or street food.
It's best to download and set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before you leave home. And you will need a working internet connection to activate them, which brings us to the next point.

5. Do You Have Internet Access?
Your phone will not automatically work in China due to the Great Firewall.
eSIM: Purchase an eSIM before you travel (apps offer China coverage). This gives you data immediately upon landing.
Local SIM card: Available at the airport, and remember to look for an official one, but it requires registration and can take 15–30 minutes to set up.
Roaming: Check with your home carrier. Some international plans include China coverage, but speeds may be slow.
If none of the above methods work, that's okay. You can ask airport staff for assistance in connecting to the airport's free Wi-Fi to complete all the setup and registration.
And it's best to download offline maps of Shanghai (Google Maps is not very stable in China, so Baidu Maps or Gaode Maps are recommended), and save your hotel or attraction addresses in Chinese characters to show taxi drivers.

6. Where Can You Store Your Luggage?
If you are carrying luggage, you won't want to drag it around the city.
There are five luggage deposit offices in Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 at Shanghai Pudong International Airport.
Terminal 1: Near Gate 4–5 and Gate 10–11 in Departures Hall, and near Gate 6–7 in Arrivals Hall. Tel: 021-6834 6324.
Terminal 2: Near Service Counter A and Service Counter M in Departures Hall, and near the International Arrivals exit. Tel: 021-6834 0076.
This may cost you approximately 50 RMB per day for large items, while smaller luggage is cheaper.
Our advice: Store your luggage in the same terminal you will depart from, so you can pick it up on your way to check-in without backtracking.
Final Checklist Before You Leave the Airport
Your real available city time is at least 6 hours.
You have confirmed you do not need a visa (or have one ready).
You have cash (RMB) or Alipay/WeChat Pay set up and linked to your card.
You have internet access (eSIM, SIM, or roaming).
You have stored your luggage or are traveling light.
You know your return route and have allowed enough time to get back.
If you have all six checked off, you are ready to step out and see Shanghai.

Shanghai Transportation
Shanghai, as the most modern international metropolis in China, has an all-round development of transportation system. No matter where you are now – overseas or in other parts of China – there are efficient and convenient transportation modes you can choose to reach Shanghai, and the diverse transportation options within the city also contribute to a worry-free Shanghai journey for you.
How to Get to Shanghai?
1. By Air – the Most Efficient Way to Arrive Shanghai from AfarSuppose your first destination in China is Shanghai, taking the plane is the most efficient way. Shanghai airports rank among the top three busiest airports in the world because of their wide coverage of airline service and dense air routes over the globe. At present, there are two airports in Shanghai – Pudong International Airport and Hongqiao International Airport, with the former being the major international aviation hub whose air routes are available in more than 130 cities overseas, and the latter mainly providing flight service in Chinese mainland mostly, as well as a few short-haul routes to Hong Kong, Macao, Taiwan, Japan, and South Korea.
By virtue of its extensive and convenient air service, Shanghai has constantly been the first stop when a large number of international tourists travel in China, as well as the transfer stop as many tourists travel to other Asian countries such as Japan and South Korea. Terminal of Shanghai Pudong International Airport  
2. By Bullet Train – More Efficient Option to Reach Shanghai within a Short-to-Medium Distance
Taking the bullet train can be a good choice if you depart from other Chinese cities to Shanghai, since 97% of Chinese cities have direct high-speed rail connections to Shanghai. In particular, if your departure location is not too far away from Shanghai, taking the high-speed train will be more cost-effective. For instance, it only takes 30 - 60 minutes to arrive Shanghai from its neighboring cities in the Yangtze River Delta including Suzhou, Hangzhou, Nanjing, etc. and 4 - 5 hours from central part of China like Beijing.
Nevertheless, the train journey from farther cities such as Xi'an and Chongqing to Shanghai takes a relatively longer time, approximately 6 hours. Therefore, if you have a tight schedule, you are more recommended to travel by air, which allows you to arrive in 2 - 2.5 hours.
Among all railway stations in the city, Shanghai Railway Station is the closest one to Shanghai downtown, followed by Shanghai South Railway Station and Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station. They all link with metro lines that can reach the Bund in 20 - 40 minutes. Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station Shanghai Bullet Trains

How to Get Around in Shanghai?
The transportation within Shanghai is efficient and travelers-friendly as well, with a diverse range of public transportation modes for you to pick. Subway, buses, and taxis all provide real-time service for you to visit various attractions and commercial districts; feature transportation like ferries, sightseeing buses, and the bund sightseeing tunnel are also available, not only facilitating your itinerary but also offering you unique experience to appreciate beautiful views of Shanghai.
1. By Metro
Taking the metro is preferred for traveling around Shanghai, especially during morning and evening peak hours on weekdays, which helps you in avoiding the traffic congestion on the roads. Besides, taking the subway is very economical, with a one-way fare ranging from CNY 3 to 26.
Currently, there are 20 metro lines in service in Shanghai. They not only connect the two airports and major railway stations, but also cover most attractions and commercial districts. For example, Shanghai Metro Line 1 links Hongqiao International Airport, Hongqiao Railway Station and the Bund; and Line 2 passes through Pudong International Airport, Shanghai Railway Station, Shanghai South Railway Station, People's Square, as well as Shanghai Tower, etc. Train Platform of Shanghai Metro Line 2 Inside Shanghai Metro Train

2. By Taxi Shanghai Taxi If you are burdened with luggage, then taking the taxi would be more comfortable, since taking the subway involves going back and forth between the station halls and the train platforms, as well as going through luggage security checks, which are inevitable. Moreover, traveling by taxi is one of the recommended modes during off-peak hours. Whether hailing a taxi by the roadside or ordering a taxi ride through the online platform, you can quickly board the vehicle and set off. Particularly if you want to visit some scenic spots in the suburbs of Shanghai, such as Zhujiajiao Water Town, whose entrance to the scenic area is about 15 minutes' walk from the nearest subway station, taking a taxi can send you directly to the destination.
The taxi fare in Shanghai is relatively reasonable. The cost for traveling between attractions within the city is approximately CNY 40 - 80 (USD 6 - 12), while the fare for going to the airport or more distant suburban attractions is about CNY 150 - 300 (USD 23 - 45). 
3. By BusThere are diverse buses in Shanghai, including City Bus, Sightseeing Bus, Tourist Bus, as well as Long-distance Bus.
City Bus: The city buses have extensive routes covering almost everywhere around Shanghai, serving as an alternative to the metro and perfectly making up for the inconvenience of some subway stations being a bit far from the entrance of certain tourist attractions. During off-peak hours, you may consider traveling by city bus, which helps you save money and also offer you convenience to admire the street views along the journey.
Sightseeing Bus: Shanghai boasts a lot of sightseeing buses, allowing you to visit the city’s landmark attractions in one go. What’s more, the buses are equipped with headphones that support multiple languages, making it easier for you to understand the history and fascinating stories behind different attractions. The most recommended sightseeing bus route is City Sightseeing Line 1, which connects the Bund, Lujiazui, Yu Garden, Nanjing Road, and People’s Square, among other core attractions. Shanghai Sightseeing Bus Shanghai Tourist Buses 
Tourist Bus: There are many tourist buses heading for scenic areas that are far away from the city center or located in some nearby cities. For instance, you can take the tourist bus from Shanghai South Railway Station directly to Shanghai Disneyland Park in 40 minutes at the cost of CNY 10, which is more time-saving and relaxing than taking the crowded metro. Besides, there are tourist buses from Shanghai Hongqiao Airport / Railway Station to Wuzhen Water Town operating four times each day, with the one-way ticket fare of CNY 55.
Long-distance Bus: If you are going to travel from Shanghai downtown to suburbs or its neighboring cities like Suzhou and Hangzhou, there are also long-distance buses for your consideration except for bullet trains.

As a bonus, you avoid the overcrowded entries / exits of the metro station, and gain an unforgettable experience to enjoy the fabulous river views. Additionally, if you want to visit Chongming Island, Changxing Island, or Hengsha Island near Shanghai downtown, you can also take the ferry.
The Bund Sightseeing Tunnel offers another way to travel between the two sides of the Huangpu River, providing a direct 3- to 5-minute passage to the opposite bank. As you go through the tunnel, you’ll experience fascinating visual and auditory effects that create a sensation of traveling through time and space. However, it’s somewhat more expensive, with a one-way fare of CNY 50.

How to Pay for Public Transportation in Shanghai?
1. Using NFC PaymentIf your phone supports NFC payment, you can add your bank card such as UnionPay chip cards, Visa cards, Mastercards, American Express, and JCB contactless cards to the Wallet application such Apple Pay on your phone. Just make sure that there is sufficient balance in your card and that low-value password-free payment works, and then you can pay for metro ride by swiping NFC.
2. By CashCash is the most direct way to pay for public transportation in Shanghai. However, you are advised to prepare some change in advance since payment for the bus fee is usually in a self-help way, CNY 2 per trip. As for buying metro tickets, there are self-service ticket machines in the station halls that accept only banknotes of denominations of CNY 5, 10, and 20. You can also buy the metro ticket at the manual service counter, which is available in every metro station hall.
3. Using Shanghai Public Transportation Card Shanghai Metro Three-Day Pass You can use Shanghai Public Transportation Card in any transportation scenario, including taking the metro and bus, and even the ferry and Shanghai taxi. The card comes in two varieties, one is purple that can only be used in Shanghai, and the other is red that is valid nationwide. Both cost CNY 20 each, and can be purchased in most metro stations.
4. Using Metro Day PassOne-day Travel Pass and Three-day Travel Pass are two types of metro tickets mostly suitable for travelers. They can be purchased both at self-service ticket machines and the manual service counters in most subway stations.
The One-day Travel Pass costs CNY 18 and can be used unlimited times within 24 hours; the Three-day Travel Pass costs CNY 45 and can be used unlimited times within 72 hours. You may decide which ticket to buy based on your stay in Shanghai.
5. WeChat / Alipay QR Code ScanningIf you have a WeChat or Alipay account with proper payment functionality, you will find it much easier to use any mode of transportation. First, add the ride codes for Shanghai Metro and city buses on the WeChat Ride mini-program or in the Transport section of Alipay. Then, simply open the corresponding ride code before boarding to pay the fare. For taking taxis, you can choose to order one online in the travel service section of the ride application, or scan the driver’s QR code after arriving at your destination for payment. Top 8 Shopping Areas in Shanghai
Shanghai, famed as the “Shopping Paradise” and the “Oriental Paris”, offers an irresistible allure that makes shopping an essential and unmissable part of any visit. Be it the pursuit of high-end luxury labels, the discovery of traditional Chinese souvenirs, or the immersion in the vibrant atmosphere of local markets, this metropolis unfailingly delivers a shopping escapade that is both superlative and all-encompassing.
1. Nanjing Road - The Most Prosperous Shopping Street
Nanjing Road is one of the most bustling shopping streets in Shanghai, stretching over five kilometers in length and divided into two parts. The eastern part is dotted with specialty stores and diminutive shops that offer a wide array of clothing and electronic items. On the west side, you'll find upscale shopping malls and flagship stores of international luxury brands such as Cartier and Dior. Along this vibrant thoroughfare, numerous cafes and restaurants are conveniently situated, affording weary shoppers a welcome respite. Additionally, there are tram lines running from one end of the street to the other, providing an energy-saving way to go shopping.

2. Huaihai Road - Luxury Goods Gathering Place
Huaihai Road, a quintessential high-end fashion shopping boulevard in Shanghai, is home to a sprawling array of over 400 stores. It serves as a magnet for a multitude of international luxury brands. Renowned names such as Gucci, Chanel, and Louis Vuitton have established their flagship emporiums here, presenting the latest and trendiest collections of clothing, bags, shoes, and other fashionable accouterments of the current season.
3. Yuyuan Bazaar - The Grand Bazaar of Traditional Features
Yuyuan Bazaar, being one of Shanghai's historical and cultural landmarks, exhibits a distinctively unique Chinese traditional style. It houses a diverse range of Chinese-style commodities and handicrafts, accompanied by numerous food stalls. Here, visitors can purchase souvenirs that are rich in Chinese characteristics, such as silk products, antiques, jade, and traditional handicrafts, thereby enabling them to immerse themselves in and experience the alluring charm of Chinese traditional culture.  Yuyuan Bazaar, Shanghai An exquisite store in Tianzifang 
4. Tianzifang - The Fusion of Art and Shopping
Tianzifang, nestled in the French Concession of Shanghai, is a charming district where many edifices preserve the architectural essence of the 1930s. Comprising a labyrinth of narrow and winding alleys, it houses a profusion of art studios, galleries, and specialty boutiques. Here, one can relish the sight of avant-garde paintings, sculptures, photographic masterpieces, and other forms of artistic expression, while also having the opportunity to acquire handicrafts, exquisite jewelry, T-shirts emblazoned with artistic motifs, and other unique commodities. Moreover, it serves as a haven for photography aficionados, as even a spontaneous click can yield a photograph replete with artistic charm.
5. Xintiandi - The Trendy Place Blending the Old and the New
Xintiandi, a remarkable blend of tradition and modernity, as well as an integration of the East and the West. The northern block preserves the time-honored Shikumen buildings, while the southern block infuses new urban vitality. Surrounding the area, prosperous edifices host a variety of establishments such as gourmet restaurants, cozy book bars, exotic cafes, and high-end shopping malls. The nightlife here is also very rich. There are many bars and entertainment venues, which are good places for you to relax and feel the vitality of modern urban Shanghai after shopping. Xintiandi, Shanghai Prcelains in the Yunzhou Curio City 
6. Yunzhou Curio City - The Treasure Hunting Spot for Antique Collections
The Yunzhou Curio City, a significant antique trading market in Shanghai, houses hundreds of stalls brimming with a diverse range of antique treasures. Within it, one can discover antique porcelain pieces hailing from various epochs and exhibiting an array of shapes. Vintage cameras are also present, bearing the photographic memories of bygone times, and old vinyl records that enable one to savor the charm of the music from years past.
7. AP Plaza – A Paradise for Budget Shoppers
The AP Plaza is renowned for its budget-friendly offerings. The stalls are densely populated with an extensive assortment of merchandise. Here, one can encounter “substitute” items of clothing in the styles of various prominent brands, sporting diverse designs that stay in tune with the latest fashion fads. Despite their lack of authenticity, the prices are highly accessible. If the genuineness of the products is not a concern for you and you place greater emphasis on cost-effectiveness and distinctive aesthetics, then exploring this market for a shopping spree will prove to be an engaging and enjoyable experience.
8. The South Bund Fabric Market - The Ideal Place for Custom-Made Clothing South Bund Fabric Market, Shanghai 
The South Bund Fabric Market stands as an optimal destination for bespoke clothing. It presents a rich selection of fabrics, such as silk, chiffon, leather, and others, which vary in both material and color. Be it an elaborate dress, a trendy suit, or a traditional Chinese cheongsam or gown, the proficient tailors here are capable of customizing it to your precise specifications. Bargaining is a common practice in this market. You can engage in price negotiation with the tailors and thereby obtain satisfactory custom-made clothing at a more economical price.

Yu Garden
 Yu Garden, aka Yuyuan Garden, Yu Yuan in Chinese, is a classical garden ranking among the must-see attractions in Shanghai. It dates back to the Ming Dynasty (1368-1644) and was owned by a government officer named Pan Yunduan. Yu in Chinese means pleasing and satisfying, and this garden was specially built for Pan’s parents as a place for them to enjoy a tranquil and happy time in their old age. Pond and Cloister of Yu Garden

Facts about Yu GardenLocation: No. 137, Anren Street, Huangpu District, very close to the Old City God Temple
Area: 20,000 square meters (about 5 acres)
Time Needed for a Visit: about 2 hours
History of Yu Garden Yuyuan Garden Map (Click to Enlarge) This garden was not constructed all at once since its inception in 1559. Instead, it was built in intermittent phases over nearly 20 years before the completion in 1590. After Pan Yunduan’s death, his family fell into decline, so the garden was poorly maintained and changed hands several times. 
In 1760, to prevent the further decline of this garden, local wealthy merchants and gentry pooled funds to purchase Yu Garden and donated it to the Old City God Temple  nearby. The restoration of the garden took over 20 years, involving the reconstruction of pavilions and the addition of rockeries, among other efforts. Since the Old City God Temple already had an “East Garden” at the time, Yu Garden was named the “West Garden” to complement it. Later, Yu Garden was severely damaged again in wars. In 1956, the Shanghai government initiated restoration efforts, renamed the Old City God Temple’s “East Garden” as “Inner Garden”, and incorporated it into Yu Garden. In 1961, the Yu Garden was then officially opened to the public.
What to See in Yu Garden?Although the scale of Yu Garden is not too large, it contains all elements that a classical Chinese garden should have—the pavilions, halls, rockeries, carvings, ponds and cloisters, all of which have unique characteristics. Here are some highlights in the garden that you would not want to miss.  Pavillion and Pond in Yu Garden Exquisite Carvings in Yu Garden  
Great RockeryUpon entering Yu Garden, you will encounter a field of rockery which is called the Great Rockery. With a height of 14 meters (about 50 feet), it’s the largest as well as the oldest one in the southern region of the Yangtze River. Standing at the small pavilion on the top of it, you can get a bird’s eye view of the whole garden.
Sansui HallSansui Hall features five bays, standing at a height of 9 meters. It’s the most commodious structure in the garden. This Hall was originally called Leshou by Pan, which means joy and longevity, and was used to entertain guests. Later in the Qing Dynasty (1636-1912), it was renovated and renamed as “Sansui”, symbolizing prosperous official career and the proper utilization of talents, as well as the people’s aspiration for bountiful harvests and favorable weather conditions. It served as a venue for official ceremonies, the dissemination of imperial edicts, and gatherings of local gentry. Elegant Furniture in Yu Garden  
Wanhua ChamberWanhua Chamber is a two-story delicate building surrounded by devious cloisters. Its architectural details all carry a profound cultural significance: the fence is engraved with patterns of Chinese legends’ magical weapons and the traceries of the windows at the four corners of the ground floor are carvings in plum, orchid, bamboo and chrysanthemum shapes, respectively, symbolizing the presence of flowers throughout four seasons. In front of the Chamber stand two old trees, one being the 21-meter-tall (about 70-foot) ginkgo tree said to have been planted by the owner of Yu Garden over 400 years ago, and the other being the southern magnolia tree with a history of more than 100 years. Pavilions of Yu Garden  
Dianchun HallDianchun Hall, built later in the Qing Dynasty, was a place where families gathered for feasts on festive occasions. If you look at the details of this area, you will find there are many sculptures with the meaning of warding off evil spirits. In front of the hall, there is a water-facing opera stage. To the west is the “Cloud-Piercing Dragon Wall” featuring a clay dragon head with a tiled dragon body. This unique type of design is highly recognizable since it’s rarely seen in gardens of southern region of the Yangtze River. Cloud-Piercing Dragon Wall  
Yuhua Hall“Yuhua” means the “magnificence of the jade”, so Yuhua Hall is also known as “Jade Magnificence Hall”. It is a graceful constructure with furnishings made of top-grade rosewood and was originally the study of Pan where he read books and met friends. Stepping inside, you can still see Pan’s precious items such as the Four Treasures of the Study——writing brush, ink stick, paper, and ink stone, and the purple sandalwood drawing table.
Exquisite Jade RockThe true treasure of Yu Garden is the Exquisite Jade Rock, one of the three most famous rockeries in south China. It’s 3.3 meters (about 10.8 feet) in height and has 72 holes. What is interesting about this rock is that if you burn a joss stick from its bottom, the smoke will magically float out from all the holes. Similarly, while you pour water into the rock from its top, the water will flow out from each hole, creating a spectacular sight to see. Yuhua Hall, located opposite the Exquisite Jade Rock, was specifically built for the purpose of appreciating this wonderful artifact. Exquisite Jade Rock  
Inner GardenThe Inner Garden is long and narrow, but it is a very exquisite "garden within a garden". When entering this area, the first thing that catches your eye is the Hall of Serenity. Sitting in the hall, you can calmly observe the numerous strange peaks and rocks on the opposite side. Next is the Pavilion of Watching Waves. As the name suggests, when you climb up this pavilion and look eastward, you can see the waves of the Huangpu River. The most special feature is the Ancient Opera Stage. Its top is formed by the intersection of 22 circles and 20 arcs, which enables the singing to produce a lingering sound effect. In addition, there is a mirror on the top of the stage, and around it are 28 golden birds spreading their wings, creating a magnificent and exquisite visual effect.
What to Do in Yu Garden Area?Apart from admiring those marvelous architectures, you have a lot of other interesting things to do around Yu Garden.
Stroll Around the Yuyuan BazaarYuyuan Bazaar, on the periphery of Yu Garden, is a lively marketplace where all kinds of traditional craftworks, souvenirs, and food stalls are displayed in abundance. What a fulfilled journey to stroll along this street after exploring the garden! As a friendly reminder, bargaining is necessary. The Commercial Street  
Appreciate the Night View on the Nine Bend BridgeThe zigzag Nine Bend Bridge is a part of Yu Garden, but outside the area that charges entrance fee. At night, colorful lights on both sides of the bridge are attractive. During the Chinese New Year, lantern exhibitions will be held there, adding more charm to the night piece.
Taste Local DelicaciesNear Yu Garden, there are many highly-rated century-old restaurants. If you don’t feel like leaving soon, you might as well try the flavors of Shanghai! The first one is Nanxiang Steamed Buns , a well-known Chinese traditional brand, famous for its soft and juicy soup-filled buns, which is definitely worth tasting. Then there is Lubolang Restaurant, where you can enjoy imperial-level delicacies such as osmanthus rice cake, crystal shrimp, and butterfly pastry. Shanghai Old Restaurant and Dexing Restaurant are famous for their Shanghai local cuisine and have remained popular for a long time. Chunfeng Songyue is the oldest vegetarian restaurant in Shanghai, making it perfect for vegetarian tourists.
Yuyuan Lantern FestivalThe Yuyuan Lantern Festival originated during the Ming and Qing dynasties and has been resumed since 1995. Every year, before and after the Lunar New Year, various folk culture activities with different themes are held in the radiation area of Yu Garden. The Yuyuan Lantern Festival in 2026 will be themed around “Chinese zodiac horse” and will last for 36 days from January 26th to March 3rd. The main venue is Yuyuan Bazaar. During this period, the streets and alleys of this area are adorned with various horse-shaped lanterns. At the Yuyuan Golden Square, digital light projection technology presents scenes of traditional New Year customs, creating an exceptionally festive atmosphere.
Note: During the lantern festival, it is free to enter the venue before 16:00 from Monday to Thursday, and from Friday to Sunday as well as on February 17th and March 3rd before 14:00. For other time periods, the charge is CNY 80 per adult, and CNY 50 per child aged under 11 or under 1.4 meters (4.6 feet). Additionally, the venue will be closed on New Year’s Eve on February 16th. 
Best Time to Visit Yu GardenThe best times to visit Yu Garden are late spring from April to May and Autumn from September to November. During the two periods, you can enjoy a harmonious combination of the garden’s own charm with its natural surroundings. In late spring, the garden is embellished with diversified flowers in full bloom as well as vigorously green willows. As autumn drives summer heat away, it becomes distinctively mesmerizing with the foliage turning colorful. Additionally, approximately from late December to February in winter is the best time to catch the grand Lantern Festival around Yu Garden where various Chinese Lanterns light up, enhancing its antique and lively ambience.
Time to avoid visiting: Spring Festival in late January to mid-February, May Day Holiday from May 1st to May 5th, and National Day Holiday from October 1st to October 7th. During these official public holidays in China, there is often a large number of visitors to the Garden, leading to crowdedness of the scenic spots. Yu Garden in Late Spring
