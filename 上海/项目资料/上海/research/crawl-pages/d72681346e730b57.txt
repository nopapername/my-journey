Shanghai Itinerary Planning Guide: How Many Days Do You Need
 Written by
 Carper
 Updated Aug. 6, 2026 
Most first-time visitors wonder the same thing when planning Shanghai: if adding another day to Shanghai means taking one away from another destination, is it really worth it?
For a rather comprehensive trip, we'd like to recommend having three days in Shanghai, so you will have enough time to experience the city instead of feeling like you're checking off a list.
Instead of completing a mission of moving from one landmark to the next, we would like to take you to unfold the everyday moments that make Shanghai one of the most memorable stops on your first journey in China.

Why We Strongly Recommend a Three-Day Stay in Shanghai
Most first-time visitors wonder: can't I see Shanghai's highlights in one day? Technically yes, but you may leave feeling like you only saw the cover of the book.
One or two days let you see Shanghai. Three days let you experience it.
We would like to describe Shanghai in three layers:
The historic Bund and old city streets.
The leafy, everyday life of the Former French Concession.
The futuristic energy of Pudong.
Each layer deserves its own day, so you can slow down, wander, and actually feel the contrast between them. 
Follow our three-day plan that takes you through all three layers—one day for the past, one for the present, and one for the future—at a pace that leaves room for discovery, not just checklist-ticking.
Still haven't decided when to visit Shanghai? See Best Times to Visit Shanghai to find the period that suits you the most.
Day 1: A Glimpse Into Shanghai's Past
The International Architecture Exhibition at the Bund, Shanghai
Start your morning on the Bund, walking along the historic waterfront with a guide who will bring the old buildings to life—from the grand Astor House Hotel to the iconic Peace Hotel and the former HSBC Building.
To help you create lasting memories, we offer postcards and stamps for you to choose from, so you can send a piece of the Bund home to someone special.
After lunch near Nanjing Road, continue your day to Yu Garden, a classical Chinese garden hidden in the old city. Wander through its winding corridors, koi ponds, and bizarre rockeries, while learning about the garden's remarkable story of destruction and rebirth.
If time allows, stroll along Shanghai Old Street before returning to the Bund for sunset. We suggest arriving there 30–40 minutes early to claim a good spot on the waterfront and watch the skyline transform as the city lights begin to glow.

Day 2: Be a Part of Shanghai's Lively Present
Open-air cafes on Sinan Road in the Former French Concession of Shanghai
Your day begins beneath the plane trees of the Former French Concession. 
Start your walk at Fuxing Park, where a century ago French residents sipped coffee behind iron gates while Chinese locals could only look in from outside. Today, the park is for everyone.
Walk along Sinan Road, lined with vintage villas and quiet doorways, where your guide will share the stories of the families who once lived here. You can pause for a while at a corner café tucked inside a lilong alleyway, where modern coffee shops and bakeries now breathe new life into the old lanes.
If you are curious about Shanghai cuisine, we can arrange a cooking class at a local home—from shopping for ingredients at the market to sitting down to savor the dishes you have made.
End your walk at the Wukang Building, a ship-shaped apartment block that once housed foreign elites and now stands as a beloved landmark of modern Shanghai.

Day 3: Jump to Shanghai's Future
The Shanghai Trio at night
Today your trip will start with a ride—an autonomous one—to Shanghai Tower, the world's third-tallest building. 
Take the high-speed elevator to the 118th-floor observation deck, where the daytime panorama reveals the city buzzing with life below: cars like toys, crowds like ants.
Then your guide will lead you to step into the future at the world's first retail store operated by Gal, an embodied intelligent robot. Place your order and watch as the robot handles everything. 
If you have extra time, try ordering a takeaway delivered by drone, which typically arrives within fifteen minutes.
End your day at the DJI or Huawei flagship store, where you can test the latest drones, gadgets, and electric vehicles that are already shaping how people live in Shanghai.
Your right travel length always depends on your travel style and available time.
Of course, you can always swap part of the itinerary for attractions that match your interests, such as Shanghai Disneyland and Zhujiajiao Water Town, but you may inevitably lose some understanding of Shanghai.
Whatever you choose, this guide will help you build an itinerary around what matters most to you, not just what is popular on social media. Shanghai is not a place to rush through, but a city to immerse yourself in.

A Typical One-Day Itinerary (Ideal Timeline)
9 AM - 11 AM: Shanghai Tower
12 AM - 1 PM: Yu Garden
1 PM - 2 PM: Lunch Break
3 PM - 4 PM: The Former French Concession (With a limited day trip, you can only visit Wukang Road, and skip Sinan Park and Fuxing Park to miss a deeper understanding of Shanghai's colonial history.)
5 PM - 6 PM: The Bund (We believe the Bund is more worthwhile to spend 2 hours walking around, as you can truly step into the historical buildings and see firsthand what the Chinese have accomplished in this economic center. However, due to the limited time on a day trip, only a simple walk can be well-arranged.)
This itinerary covers four must-see sights with a well-planned schedule, and adding more is simply not feasible. As we have already considered the queues and traffic with a stress-free private transfer, you will be in good hands traveling with us.
For a self-guided day trip, taking a taxi or metro is workable, but you're very likely to encounter traffic jams or overcrowding during midday and afternoon rush hours, which may lead to a delay of subsequent sightseeing.
To enjoy a smooth one-day trip, consider starting your day at 8:00 AM to beat the crowds and avoid rush hours. Alternatively, you can also cut one attraction that you find less appealing for a more relaxed pace.

Two Days in Shanghai: Focus On the Core Sights
Explore the tree-lined Former French Concession of Shanghai
Having two days in Shanghai means you have time to feel the city from two distinct layers, but you still can't afford to squeeze all the highlights or even a side trip from Shanghai city center without sacrificing experiences or the relaxed pace.
The Bund and the Former French Concession, representing the past and lively present, are both excellent candidates for this kind of immersive experience.
On Day One, you can walk the Bund, step inside the grand buildings of the "International Architecture Exhibition," and uncover the stories behind them. After lunch, continue to the old city and Yu Garden—all within walking distance—and return to the Bund before 6 PM for a stunning sunset view of the skyline, if the weather is clear.
On Day Two, immerse yourself in the leafy streets of the Former French Concession. Walk along Sinan Road, pass by the legendary mansions that once belonged to Shanghai's most powerful figures, and stop at Tianzifang's maze-like alleyways, where traditional lilong housing now hosts boutiques, galleries, and cafés.
If you are more interested in the futuristic side of Shanghai, feel free to replace one day that may seem less appealing to you, to enjoy the panoramic views of the urban area from Shanghai Tower's peak and high-tech gadgets at Pudong.
Should You Spend More Than 3 Days in Shanghai?
For most travelers, three days is enough to cover Shanghai's highlights without feeling rushed. But if you have four or five days, you have the luxury of going deeper.
Extra days allow you to hop on Shanghai's galleries, take a day trip to a nearby water town like Zhujiajiao or Tongli, or simply spend an unhurried afternoon in a neighborhood park watching locals go about their daily routines.
Families with children might use an extra day for Shanghai Disneyland, Legoland Shanghai, or the Science and Technology Museum.

If Shanghai has satisfied you and you want to explore further, a day trip to Suzhou or Hangzhou is recommended.
