Shanghai Yu Garden: Tips for What to See & How to Visit
 Written by
 Carper
 Updated Sep. 10, 2026 
Yu Garden, also known as Yuyuan (豫园), is worth visiting on your first Shanghai trip, especially if Suzhou (known for classic Jiangnan gardens) is not on your itinerary. As a first-timer, you should allow 1–2 hours for the garden.
The biggest misunderstanding is that many travelers mix up Yu Garden with the Yuyuan Bazaar outside it.
Inside the garden, you will get a compact introduction to classical Chinese garden design: rockeries, ponds, moon gates, pavilions, and views that change as you walk.
Outside, the Yuyuan Bazaar is a very different experience: busy, commercial, photogenic, and convenient for Shanghai snacks. Add another 1–2 hours if you want to eat and explore the surrounding bazaar.

Inside Yu Garden: Focus on Rockeries, Water, and Framed Views
The garden is the historic core, and it rewards slow observation.
Don't treat it as a checklist of buildings. The main attraction is how the landscape changes as you move through it.
The Grand Rockery in Yu Garden
Grand Rockery and Water
The Grand Rockery (大假山) is one of the garden's defining features. 
Instead of viewing it from one fixed point, walk around it and look at how the rocks, trees, walls, and water interact. The garden's ponds and water features make the rockery feel larger and more layered than the compact site actually is.
Moon Gates frame the scenery of Yu Garden
Moon Gates and Changing Views
Look through the round moon gates and lattice windows rather than simply walking past them.
A doorway may frame a pavilion.
A wall may hide a pond until you turn the corner.
A narrow passage may suddenly open into a courtyard.
This "view within a view" is one of the best reasons to visit with a guide. 
Our guide will explain with stories why certain openings, rocks, roof decorations, and sightlines were designed the way they were, instead of leaving you to photograph them without context.
Dragon Walls in Yu Garden
Dragon Walls and Traditional Details
Look up as you walk.
The curved dragon walls are among the garden's most recognizable details, with dragon figures running along the tiled walls and rooflines.
These details are easy to miss when the paths are crowded, which is another reason to visit early.

Explore Yuyuan Bazaar: Food, Photos, and Atmosphere
Step outside the garden walls and you're in a very different space. 
The Yuyuan Bazaar is a modern commercial area built in traditional Chinese architectural style. Its curved roofs, upturned eaves, red-and-gold facades, shops, and lanterns create the "Old Shanghai" look seen in many travel photos.
It is visually convincing, but don't mistake the buildings for the historic garden itself.
Go Here for Shanghai Snacks and "Old Shanghai" Vibe
The area is convenient for trying popular Shanghai foods such as:
- Nanxiang Steamed Bun Restaurant: xiaolongbao (soup dumplings)
- Da Hu Chun: shengjian (pan-fried buns)
- Lu Bo Lang: crab roe pastries
- Tanghulu (candied hawthorn berries)
Spots like Nanxiang Steamed Bun Restaurant are one of the area's best-known names, but don't automatically spend your limited sightseeing time waiting in a long queue simply because a restaurant is famous.
If your priority is food rather than the restaurant name, use the bazaar as a chance to sample one or two local snacks and keep moving.
Shanghai Yu Garden and City God Temple Lantern Festival
Go Here for Evening Photos
The bazaar is particularly photogenic after dark, when lanterns and illuminated facades create the traditional-style Shanghai scene many visitors expect from the area.
That makes the combination especially efficient: Garden in the afternoon → bazaar later → evening meal or Bund at night.
Tip: Add Shanghai City God Temple If You Have 15–30 Minutes
Just outside the south gate of Yu Garden, Shanghai City God Temple (上海城隍庙) is a small Taoist temple dedicated to the city's protective deities.
Entry is free, and the temple is generally open from 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM.
If you are interested in Chinese folk religion or Taoist traditions, step inside and take a look at the incense offerings and religious atmosphere.
If not, don't make a special trip just for the temple. Its traditional architecture is easy to appreciate from outside, and your limited time is better spent inside Yu Garden or exploring the surrounding bazaar.
Best use of your time: Treat it as a 15–30-minute add-on while exploring Yuyuan, not as a separate Shanghai attraction.

When Is the Best Time to Visit Yu Garden?
For the Garden: 9:00–10:00 AM
Yuyuan Garden in the morning
The garden's current regular opening hours are 9:00 AM–4:30 PM, with last entry at 4:00 PM. It is normally closed on Mondays except on statutory holidays.
If the garden itself is your priority, aim close to 9:00 AM.
This matters most on weekends and public holidays, when tour groups and domestic travelers fill the narrow paths by mid-morning. 
Arriving early gives you two advantages: softer light for photos and enough space to stop, look, and appreciate the garden's details without being pushed along by the crowd.
For the Bazaar: Late Afternoon to Evening
Shanghai's Yuyuan Garden Bazaar light up after nightfall.
The bazaar is at its best in the evening, around 7 PM, when the lights come on across the traditional buildings, lanterns glow along the streets, and the area becomes lively with shops, food stalls, and visitors.
On most days, the bazaar is free to enter, so you do not need to buy a Yu Garden ticket just to enjoy the illuminated "Old Shanghai" atmosphere.
There is one important exception: from January to March, the annual Lantern Festival is held around the Jiuqu Bridge and Huxinting (the central area of the bazaar). During the festival, this area requires a separate entrance ticket, so the usual free-entry arrangement does not apply.
For the exact Lantern Festival dates, ticket information, and any other special events taking place during your visit, check the WeChat mini-program "上海豫园" before you go.

Can I Visit Yu Garden at Night?
Night tour tickets: ¥150 per person for a Chinese-guided tour; ¥190 per person for an English-guided tour
Night-tour hours:
- May–October: 6:30 PM–9:50 PM, Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday
- January–April & November–December: 6:00 PM–9:20 PM, Friday, Saturday and Sunday
Yes, but for a first visit, we recommend seeing the garden by day and saving the evening for the Yuyuan Bazaar.
The night tour is a separate, paid guided experience available only on specific dates. You follow a fixed route through the garden and watch a light-and-shadow show, with the visit lasting about 45 minutes.
That format is the main reason we don't recommend it for most first-time visitors.
You cannot explore the garden at your own pace. If you want to stop at a moon gate, photograph the rockeries from different angles, or linger beside a pond, the fixed route leaves you much less freedom than a daytime visit.
Our Pick
For ¥150–190 per person, the experience makes the most sense if you specifically want the light-and-shadow show or an evening guided experience.
Otherwise, visit Yu Garden during the day when you can explore the classical garden at your own pace.
Then, after dark, skip the paid garden entry and walk around the Yuyuan Bazaar instead. The surrounding area is generally free to enter and becomes especially atmospheric when the traditional-style buildings and lanterns are illuminated.
Only have 2–3 hours in this part of Shanghai? We can build Yu Garden into a route with the bazaar and the Bund, so you can see the highlights without walking back and forth.
1-Day Shanghai Itinerary
Morning: Explore the Former French Concession by strolling along the streets lined with leafy plane trees.
Afternoon: Admire the classical Yu Garden for its curved bridges and delicate pavilions. The bazaar outside the garden is also worth your time.
Night: Arrive at the Bund before sunset for the spectacular skyline.

How to Get to Yu Garden: Metro Is Usually Easiest
Yu Garden sits in the heart of Shanghai's old city, making it easy to reach by metro, taxi, or on foot from nearby attractions.
By Metro
Take Shanghai Metro Line 10 or Line 14 to Yuyuan Garden Station.
The station serves the Yu Garden area, and the remaining walk to the garden entrance takes roughly 10 minutes.
For the exact exit and walking route on your travel day, follow the current station signs you see, rather than relying on an old exit number.
By Taxi or Ride-Hailing
Taxi or ride-hailing is convenient if you are traveling with children, older relatives, or luggage.
But the streets nearby can become congested, especially during busy periods.
If you are already near the Bund, walking can be more enjoyable than taking a car when traffic is heavy.

Is Yuyuan Garden Suitable for Wheelchair Visitors?
It is possible for wheelchair users to visit Yu Garden by day, but we do not recommend it.
Your sightseeing area will be severely restricted. The narrow paths, steps, stone bridges, slopes, rockeries, and uneven surfaces can make parts of the garden difficult to access, let alone the crowds. 
The Yuyuan Bazaar area outside is generally easier to navigate, but crowds can also become the bigger problem.
Traveling with a wheelchair user or someone who cannot walk for long? Tell us their mobility level when planning your Shanghai route, and we can choose the accessible sections and avoid building the day around difficult crossings.
