Shanghai Weather in February
 Shanghai suffers from damp and relatively cold air in February. Rainy days account for more than 10 days on average, but the good news is that there are rarely heavy or long-term rains. As long as you can endure a little low temperature, you can have a relatively good tour in February, when Shanghai is surrounded by decorations for the Chinese New Year and flowers like plum blossoms.
TemperatureAs a winter month in Shanghai, February features relatively cold weather. On most days, the daytime has a temperature range of 6-15 degrees Celsius (43-59 degrees Fahrenheit), and the nighttime experiences a colder climate with the temperature of 0-10°C (32-50°F).  Cold February 
The temperature in February is somewhat unstable. The first half of the month may have very cold days, though in rare instances, with daily highs dipping below 5°C (41°F) and lows potentially falling below 0°C (32°F). Conversely, the latter half of the month occasionally sees quite warm days with daily highs exceeding 20°C (68°F).
All in all, you are recommended to pay attention to the weather forecast and bring enough clothes to deal with the temperature fluctuations.
Apparent Temperature May Be Lower.Sometimes, the apparent temperature is much lower than the weather forecast tells. On the one hand, the humidity is relatively high and averages at around 71%. As a result, the air feels uncomfortably clammy and colder. On the other hand, winds sweep Shanghai occasionally in February, which tend to be stronger in riverside areas like the Bund, making the apparent temperature 3-5°C (5-9°F) lower.
PrecipitationThere are about 12 rainy days on average in Shanghai in February. However, you should not be discouraged by this number. Actually, the monthly precipitation averages only 60 mm, and the rain is usually light or lasts for a short period, meaning that it won’t influence your trip a lot. Sleet may fall this month, but only with a small chance. 
Days with or after precipitation are usually colder, so wear more if you go out for a visit on those days. Colder Rainy Days of Shanghai in February  
Daylight Hours & Sunshine HoursDaylight hours are in an increasing trend in February, but relatively short: they are about 10.4 hours and 11.3 hours, respectively, at the beginning and the end of the month. Since the sun sets quite early at around 17:29-17:52, getting up early is advised if you want to have a rich trip. Alternatively, you can extend your time on the daily tour by arranging some night visits. Anyway, the night views of Shanghai are awesome and the Bund is always on the top list. 
Generally, rainy and overcast moments occupy many hours of the daytime, and the sunshine hours are only 4 on average per day. The good news is that the sunshine is soft at this time, and you can enjoy outings without any sun-protection measures.

What to Wear in Shanghai in February? Tops: Given the changeable climate of Shanghai in February, clothes of various thicknesses are needed. In detail, you are recommended to bring long-sleeved T-shirts and shirts; hoodies and knitwear; thick jackets like padded leather jackets and wool coats; and thin down jackets. 
On most daytime hours, long-sleeved shirts, hoodies, plus padded leather jackets; or long-sleeved T-shirts, knitwear, plus wool coats are suitable and comfortable to wear. During the daytime hours on some quite warm days, the outermost layer may need to be taken off. Then, during the nighttime hours, down jackets are generally necessary.
Bottoms: For pants, thick jeans and sweatpants are both suitable choices. Long johns are useful for activities like taking the Huangpu River cruise at night. The shoes you bring should be warm and comfortable for walking, so you can bring various thick flats and sneakers. 
Others: Always take rain gear with you in case it rains. Hats and scarves are better to be packed.
What Is Shanghai Like in February?
In 2027, both the Chinese New Year and the Lantern Festival will fall in February. At this time, Shanghai finds itself enveloped in the lively festive atmosphere. In addition, some flowers begin to bloom. 
1. Yu Garden
Within the classical Garden, flowers like daffodils and plum blossoms compete to bloom. Outside the Garden, numerous large-scale lanterns themed around the Chinese New Year are visible. During the Lantern Festival period, activities like lantern riddle guessing and folk performances will enrich your itinerary. Lightened Yuyuan Bazaar Yu Garden Lantern Festival 
2. Lujiazui & The Bund
Beyond the ever-impressive “World Architecture Expo” and the Oriental Pearl Tower, you can admire romantic violets blooming along the Bund in February. This month, visiting this area at night offers not only stunning cityscapes but also the chance to witness spectacular iron flower performances against the dazzling backdrop of the Oriental Pearl Tower.
3. Zhujiajiao Water Town
Red lanterns lined the bridges and rivers of the Water Town. Besides, folk markets and folk performances will deliver you some special festival experiences.
4. Shanghai Disney Resort
Castles and parks adorned with festival decorations, and New Year-themed parades and exclusive holiday performances can be admired.
Is February Crowded for a Visit?
That depends on the specific dates. 
Generally speaking, most of February is quiet with fewer tourists due to the somewhat cold weather of Shanghai. So, you will not feel crowded in most attractions, and spend a bit less on hotels.  Quiet Shanghai in February 
However, you should note that the period of the Chinese New Year holiday is when Shanghai is full of domestic visitors, which is usually in January or February. At that time, famous attractions like the Yu Garden, Nanjing Road, the Bund, etc., are quite crowded; long lines form for each item in Shanghai Disneyland Park; and hotels surge in their prices… In 2027, the holiday will fall from February 4 to 12. 
Tips for Visiting Shanghai in February
1. Try to avoid visiting Shanghai during the Chinese New Year holiday. On the one hand, the city will be crowded, and hotels will be quite expensive. On the other hand, some restaurants and malls will close temporarily. Don’t worry that you will miss the festival atmosphere - the decorations mentioned above will be available for more than one month.
2. Book your hotels and tickets for attractions in advance, after all, Shanghai is quite a popular tourist destination both at home and abroad.
