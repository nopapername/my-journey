Shanghai Weather in July 2026: Hot, Muggy, Sunny, with Tips
 Written by
 Carper
 Updated Apr. 15, 2026 
July is one of Shanghai's hottest months, comparable to August. Average temperatures range from 26–31°C (79–88°F), with highs typically peaking around 3 PM (in July 2025, it reached 38°C / 100°F).
The "plum rainy season" ends in July, so rainfall decreases compared to June, averaging 11 rainy days (about 125 mm / 4.9 in). You can expect more sunny days from July 21 to 31, but keep in mind that this period is also likely to be the hottest of the month.
What's more, typhoon activity increases toward the end of the month, so it's wise to check the weather forecast if you're traveling then.
Is It a Good Time to Visit Shanghai in July?
Summer at Guyi Garden, a Jiangnan-style garden in Shanghai
July is one of the worst times to visit Shanghai, but that doesn't mean you can't have a great trip.
It all depends on how you handle the heat, plan your days, and use a few smart strategies.
1. Don't Overschedule Daytime Activities
July days are hot. Plan outdoor sightseeing before 11:00 AM or after 5:00 PM, when temperatures are generally below 28°C (82°F) and much more comfortable.
Between 11:00 AM and 5:00 PM, stay cool indoors: visit the air-conditioned Shanghai Museum, find a nice café for afternoon tea, or head back to your hotel for a nap before exploring a nearby shopping mall.
If you must be outside during peak heat, remember to use sunscreen and drink plenty of water to avoid heatstroke.
2. Plan Your Itinerary for Weekdays
July and August are summer vacation months for Chinese students, so weekends are especially crowded as families go out together.
Weekdays, especially Tuesdays and Wednesdays, are noticeably quieter.
For popular family attractions like Shanghai Disneyland, choose a weekday and consider buying early bird tickets or VIP passes. This can save you hours of queuing (popular rides like Zootopia: Hot Pursuit can have wait times of over 2 hours).

3. Book as Early as Possible
July is peak season, so hotel and transportation rates rise sharply.
Reserve your trip and accommodation at least three months in advance for better prices. For attraction tickets, book at least 7 days ahead since many sell out quickly.
4. Will I Be Affected by the Typhoon?
Shanghai's typhoon season is mainly from July to September, with peak periods in late July and late August.
But it is not a big concern with a well-planned itinerary. Normally, typhoons only affect flights for 1–3 days. So just keep your bookings flexible, and leave some buffer time in your itinerary in case of delays.

How to Spend A Day in Shanghai in July
Morning (7–11 AM): Enjoy Outdoor Activities
Zhujiajiao Water Town, Shanghai
In July, sunrise is around 5 AM, and sunset is around 7 PM.
To avoid the sweltering heat, schedule outdoor activities as early as possible. You'll enjoy cooler temperatures and quieter attractions before the crowds and heat arrive.
Zhujiajiao Water Town: You could not miss out on "Shanghai's Venice". Stroll along cobblestone streets, admire the quaint houses, sample local delicacies, or take a boat trip through the water town.
French Concession Citywalk: Embark on a citywalk through Shanghai's colonial history, experience the blend of Chinese and Western architectural styles, and grab a ice coffee from a street vendor to refresh yourself.
Yu Garden: This 500-year-old classical Chinese garden not only showcases the fusion of Eastern aesthetics and nature, but also brings a touch of coolness to you in the sweltering summer.
Midday (11 AM–5 PM): Escape the Heat
The 360-degree transparent glass observation deck in Oriental Pearl Tower, Shanghai
The weather becomes quite hot and humid, so air-conditioned indoor attractions are your best bet.
Alternatively, stroll through a shopping mall and have afternoon tea, or return to your hotel after a light lunch to relax in air-conditioned comfort.
Consider taking a dip in the pool and waiting for the temperature to drop before heading out again.
Shanghai Museum (East Campus): As one of China's four top museums, you can admire some of the most precious historical collections, including bronzes, jades, coins, and calligraphy works from thousands of years ago.
Oriental Pearl Tower: What sets the Oriental Pearl Tower apart from other skyscrapers in Shanghai is its 360-degree transparent glass observation deck, a child-friendly VR roller coaster, and a romantic revolving restaurant, perfectly catering to the needs of all kinds of people.
Xintiandi: This is where ancient Shikumen architecture blends with modern shopping malls, and it has a richer historical heritage than other commercial districts. Stroll through this shopping area, and check out any trendy brands or popular restaurants that catch your eye.
Evening and Night (After 5 PM): Explore Vibrant Shanghai
Shanghai night market barbecue street food
As the sun sets, the summer heat fades, and city lights begin to illuminate Shanghai, the famous "city that never sleeps".
It is just waking up, ready for you to explore.
The Bund: Enjoying the beautiful night view of the famous skyline from the Bund is a must-do for all first-time visitors to Shanghai, which can be quite crowded. We suggest you head to the Sinar Mas Plaza on the North Bund to avoid the crowds, grab a chilled drink, and wait for nightfall to light up the skyline.
Sijing Night Market (泗泾夜市): Start your food adventure at Shanghai's most bustling and largest night market. Browse the dazzling array of street food and let the vibrant atmosphere fill your senses.
Huaihai Road: This is Shanghai's most down-to-earth, social, and lively bar street. You can find a bar that interests you and relax with a drink to unwind after a long day.
What to Pack and Wear in Shanghai in July
July in Shanghai is hot, humid, and rainy.
Pack lightweight, breathable clothing, like several short-sleeved T-shirts, shorts or light wide-leg pants, and a light sun-protective jacket. Choose comfortable sneakers or sandals, and opt for light-colored, quick-drying fabrics.
Remember to always carry a dual-purpose umbrella (for sun and rain) for shade and to deal with sudden showers. High SPF sunscreen, a sun hat, and sunglasses are also essential.
For extra comfort, bring a small handheld fan and a pack of wet wipes and tissues to help you stay cool.
