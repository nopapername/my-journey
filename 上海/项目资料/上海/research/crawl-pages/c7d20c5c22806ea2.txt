Zhujiajiao Water Town: How to See Its Less Touristy Side
 Written by
 Carper
 Updated Sep. 10, 2026 
If you have only 1–2 days in Shanghai, skip Zhujiajiao and focus on the Bund, Yu Garden, the Former French Concession, and the city's skyline.
But if you have 3 days or more, Zhujiajiao is worth a half-day, especially if you want a slower contrast to central Shanghai.
The trick is to avoid spending your whole visit on the main canal streets. Give yourself 3–4 hours to mix residential lanes, old gardens, stone bridges, and quiet canal views.

Is Zhujiajiao Worth Half a Day?
Zhujiajiao Water Town in summer season
Our picks:
1–2 days in Shanghai: Skip Zhujiajiao.
3 days: Optional. Go if you have already covered the main sights and want a slower half-day outside central Shanghai.
4+ days: Recommended. You have enough time to add Zhujiajiao without taking time away from Shanghai's major sights.
Yes, Zhujiajiao is a real historic town. If you want to experience a Jiangnan water town without leaving Shanghai for a full day, this is it.
However, it is not an untouched village. The main streets have souvenir shops, snack stalls, restaurants, boat rides, and plenty of visitors. That means your experience depends heavily on when you arrive and where you walk.
If you arrive on a weekday around 8:30–9:00 AM, you can start with quieter residential lanes before the busiest part of the town fills up.
If you only have one full day in Shanghai, this journey to the western suburbs is not the best use of your time.
Unsure whether Zhujiajiao fits your Shanghai itinerary? We can help you compare it with Suzhou, Hangzhou, or another water town before you give up half a day of your trip.
The Best Zhujiajiao Half-Day Route: See the Town, Not Just the Tourist Street
Route: Visitor Center → West Well Street (西井街) → Kezhi Garden (课植园) & Hexin Garden (和心园) → Fangsheng Bridge (放生桥) → Jiangnan First Tea House (江南第一茶楼) → Tai'an Bridge (泰安桥) → Lang Bridge (廊桥) → City God Temple (城隍庙) → Daqing Post Office (大清邮局)
Distance: Roughly 4–5 km (around 7,000–9,000 steps)
Time needed: About 3–4 hours at a relaxed pace
Don't try to turn it into a checklist. The quieter lanes between the landmarks are part of the experience, rather than being hurried from one crowded photo spot to another.
1. Start at West Well Street, Not Fangsheng Bridge
Alleys of the Zhujiajiao Water Town, Shanghai
When you arrive at the visitor center, don't immediately follow the crowd toward the main canal and Fangsheng Bridge.
Turn into West Well Street (西井街) first.
The atmosphere changes quickly. Instead of souvenir shops and tour groups, you'll see residential lanes, older houses, local shops, and people going about their daily routines.
If you have another 30–45 minutes, continue toward East Well Street (东井街). It is a useful extension when you want more residential scenery rather than another landmark.
2. Don't Skip the Gardens: Both Are Worth Visiting
Kezhi Garde in Zhujiajiao Water Town
The canals and bridges show you the postcard side of the town. Kezhi Garden and Hexin Garden show you how wealthy local families actually lived and how Jiangnan garden design worked. They are also a good way to step away from the busiest commercial streets.
Current prices are:
Kezhi Garden: ¥20
Hexin Garden: ¥30
Shanghai Helong Art Gallery: ¥20
Three-attraction joint ticket: ¥60
Kezhi Garden (课植园) is the larger of the two, with more space to explore and a mix of traditional Jiangnan garden design and Western-style architectural elements.
Hexin Garden (和心园) is smaller but more intimate, with its own gardens, historic buildings, and collections.
We recommend both rather than choosing between them. They are different enough that visiting one does not make the other feel repetitive, and together they give you a much better sense of Zhujiajiao beyond its canals and tourist streets.
The paid attractions are open 8:30 AM–5:00 PM. If you buy the ¥60 joint ticket online, you need to exchange it for a physical ticket by 3:00 PM.
For this route, visit the gardens before Fangsheng Bridge. That way, you see the quieter historic side first and leave the busiest canal area for the classic views later.
3. Walk to Fangsheng Bridge for the Classic Zhujiajiao View
Fangsheng Bridge in Zhujiajiao Water Town
Now head toward Fangsheng Bridge (放生桥).
Built in 1571, it is one of Zhujiajiao's defining landmarks and the town's classic viewpoint over the canal.
This is where the water-town image you've probably seen online starts to make sense: stone bridge, whitewashed houses, narrow waterways, willow trees, and wooden boats moving below.
Expect this area to be considerably busier than the residential lanes, which is totally acceptable.
You don't come here to avoid people completely—you come here for the view.

4. Stop at Jiangnan First Tea House
The Jiangnan First Tea House
After roughly 1½–2 hours of walking, take a proper break.
The Jiangnan First Tea House (江南第一茶楼) sits beside the water and makes a useful 20–30 minute pause.
Order tea, sit by the canal, and watch the boats rather than rushing straight to the next attraction.
This is one of the moments when Zhujiajiao works best: you stop sightseeing and simply watch the town move around you.
5. Cross Tai'an Bridge and Lang Bridge
Lang Bridge in Zhujiajiao Water Town
Continue toward Tai'an Bridge (泰安桥) and then Lang Bridge (廊桥).
Tai'an Bridge is where you'll find the large characters "朱家角" (Zhujiajiao) painted on a wall—the classic spot that signals you've arrived. 
Lang Bridge is a particularly good photo stop because you can frame the canal, old buildings, stone bridges, and wooden boats in one view.
If photography matters to you, this section is more rewarding than trying to collect every bridge in town.
6. Finish at City God Temple and Daqing Post Office
City God Temple in Zhujiajiao Water Town
The City God Temple (城隍庙) is a small stop rather than a major attraction. Give it 10–15 minutes if you enjoy historic religious architecture and quieter courtyards.
Then continue to Daqing Post Office (大清邮局).
This restored Qing-era post office is an easy final stop. You can buy a postcard, have it stamped with a Zhujiajiao postmark, and continue walking without adding much time to your route.
Should You Take a Boat: Treat It as an Add-On, Not a Must
Taking a boat tour at Zhujiajiao Water Town
Our recommendation:
Solo/couple: Walk first; add a boat only if the water experience is important to you.
Family/group of 4–6: A private hand-rowed boat makes more sense.
Already/plan to visit Suzhou/Hangzhou/Guilin: The walking route may give you more variety than another short water-town boat ride.
The boat ride is not a bad experience. It is simply not essential for everyone.
The current official hand-rowed boat routes cost ¥200–300 per boat, with a maximum of 6 passengers, and take about 15–20 minutes.
For a family or group of up to six, the price becomes much easier to justify because you are paying by boat rather than per person.
For a solo traveler or couple, it is a different calculation. It depends on whether you want to charter the boat alone or share it with others.
Instead of spending hours lining up for a 15-minute experience, you may get more value from standing on Fangsheng Bridge or Lang Bridge, where you can see the boats, canals, bridges, and old houses together rather than viewing the scenery from inside a short boat ride.

Is Zhujiajiao Suitable for Children and Older Travelers?
Yes, with one important warning: the streets are mostly manageable, but the stone bridges can be steep and uneven.
For a slower family visit, stay mainly around: West Well Street → central canal → Fangsheng Bridge → tea house
You do not need to climb every bridge or visit every attraction. For older travelers or anyone with limited mobility, a private transfer can also remove the unnecessary walking between the metro station and the old town.
If you're traveling with children or older family members, we can adjust the route to reduce unnecessary walking while keeping the most photogenic canal and bridge views.
When Is the Best Time to Visit Zhujiajiao: Weekday Morning Beats Weekend Crowds
For the best chance of a quieter experience, arrive around 8:30–9:00 AM on a weekday.
Start with West Well Street and the residential lanes while the town is still relatively calm.
By late morning, the main tourist streets become noticeably busier, particularly on weekends and public holidays.
If you cannot arrive in the morning, arriving after 3:00 PM can give you quieter streets and a different atmosphere. 
But there is a trade-off: the paid attractions close around 5:00 PM, and you may not have enough time to see them all. So a late arrival is better for atmosphere than for sightseeing.

How to Get to Zhujiajiao: Metro for Value, Private Transfer for Comfort
Metro Line 17: Best for budget travelers. Take Metro Line 17 to Zhujiajiao Station, and use the local scenic shuttle to the visitor center, or walk toward the old town.
Taxi or ride-hailing: Easier for small groups. This is more convenient when you are traveling with children, luggage, or several people. You avoid the transfer from the metro station and arrive closer to the old town.
Private transfer: Best when Zhujiajiao is part of a full Shanghai day. A private transfer becomes worthwhile when you want to combine Zhujiajiao with another Shanghai experience.
Private transfer is particularly useful for families or older travelers who would rather spend their limited sightseeing time walking through Zhujiajiao than navigating public transport.
If you don't want to spend part of your Shanghai day figuring out transfers, we can arrange a private tour and fit Zhujiajiao into your existing Shanghai itinerary.
